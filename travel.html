Getting to IIT Mandi

The Kamand campus lies in the Uhl valley above Mandi town, in the Himalayan foothills. The last part of every journey is by road, so plan for an overnight bus or a long drive from the plains.

Most participants travel through Chandigarh and Mandi town

IIT Mandi has no railway station or airport close by. The usual route is by air or rail to Chandigarh or Delhi, then by road along the Chandigarh–Manali highway to Mandi town, and on to the Kamand campus.

The four-lane Kiratpur–Ner Chowk section of the highway, opened in 2023–24, has shortened the drive from the plains by a few hours. Kamand is about 15–23 km from Mandi town, depending on the route and which part of the campus you are going to: roughly 40–45 minutes by road.

All distances and journey times on this page are approximate and vary with traffic, weather and road conditions.

The road route from Delhi

About 400–420 km in all. By car the drive takes roughly 8–10 hours; overnight buses take 9–12 hours.

  1. Delhi Start: ISBT Kashmere Gate for buses, or Delhi airport
  2. Chandigarh about 250 km 4–5 h from Delhi. Airport and railway station; buses leave from the Sector 43 bus stand.
  3. Mandi town about 165–200 km 4–5 h from Chandigarh via Kiratpur Sahib, Bilaspur and Ner Chowk.
  4. IIT Mandi, Kamand about 15–23 km 40–45 min from Mandi bus stand by institute bus, local bus or taxi.

Ways to reach Mandi

Allow a full day for travel each way, and book overnight buses and flights early.

By air

The nearest airport is Kullu–Manali Airport at Bhuntar (KUU), about 45–50 km from the campus (roughly 1.5 hours). It is a small valley airport with few daily flights, mainly from Delhi, which are often affected by weather.

Chandigarh airport (IXC) has many more flights and is the most reliable choice, about 170–215 km from the campus (4–6 hours by road). Kangra airport at Gaggal is another option, about 150–175 km away.

By rail

The nearest practical railheads are Kiratpur Sahib and Anandpur Sahib in Punjab, on the line from Delhi towards Una, served by the Himachal Express and the Jan Shatabdi. From there Mandi is about 3.5 hours by taxi.

Chandigarh has the most trains and onward buses and taxis. The narrow-gauge line to Joginder Nagar is not a practical way to reach Mandi.

By road

HRTC and private Volvo and sleeper buses run overnight from ISBT Kashmere Gate in Delhi to Mandi (and on to Manali), reaching Mandi early in the morning. From Chandigarh, buses leave from the Sector 43 bus stand.

Book on the HRTC website or bus booking portals. Taxis from Chandigarh or Delhi can be hired for the whole journey.

Local transport

Institute buses run between Mandi town and the Kamand campus through the day. HRTC and private buses also run to Kamand, and taxis are available outside Mandi bus stand at all hours.

The organisers have planned shuttles from Mandi town on both conference days. Timings will be published closer to the conference.

Weather in late May and early June

The conference falls in the warm, pre-monsoon season. Days in Mandi town are often hot, typically in the low to mid 30s °C, and the campus at about 1,000 m is similar or a little cooler. Evenings are milder, around 20 °C.

Short pre-monsoon showers and thunderstorms are possible. Higher places such as Prashar Lake or Shikari Devi are much colder, especially after dark.

What to bring

  • Light cotton clothing for the day
  • A light woollen layer for evenings and excursions
  • An umbrella or light rain jacket
  • Comfortable walking shoes for the hilly campus
  • Sun protection and a water bottle

Places to visit around Mandi

Distances are approximate road distances from Mandi town. Several make good half-day or one-day trips before or after the conference.

In Mandi town

Temples of Mandi

Mandi is known for its many stone temples. The Bhootnath temple to Shiva dates from the early 16th century, when the town was founded, and the Panchvaktra temple, a protected monument, stands at the confluence of the Suketi with the Beas.

about 25 km

Rewalsar Lake (Tso Pema)

A small lake at about 1,360 m, sacred to Hindus, Buddhists and Sikhs. Its shores hold Tibetan monasteries and temples, and a 37.5 m statue of Padmasambhava overlooks the town.

about 45–50 km

Prashar Lake

A high lake at about 2,730 m with views of the snow ranges. A three-tiered pagoda-style temple to the sage Parashara stands on its bank, and a floating island of vegetation drifts on the water.

about 65 km

Barot Valley

A quiet valley on the Uhl, the same river that flows past the campus. Barot grew around the Shanan hydro-electric project of the 1920s, and is known for trout angling and the Nargu wildlife sanctuary across the river.

about 90–100 km

Shikari Devi

A roofless temple on the highest peak in Mandi district, at about 3,359 m, reached by road via Janjehli and a final climb on foot. The route passes through forest and high meadows.

about 65–70 km

Kullu

The main town of the Kullu valley on the Beas, beyond the airport at Bhuntar. It is known for its shawls and handlooms and for its Dussehra festival in autumn.

near Kullu

Bijli Mahadev

A Shiva temple at about 2,460 m on a ridge above Kullu, reached by a walk of about 3 km from the road. It is named after the lightning said to strike its lingam, by tradition about once every twelve years.

about 110 km

Manali

The hill town at the head of the Kullu valley, about 50 km beyond the airport. Old Manali, the Hadimba temple and the deodar forests are the usual starting points for a visit.

Travelling in the mountains. Roads are narrow and winding, and journeys take longer than distances suggest. Allow buffer time, especially before a flight or train, and avoid driving after dark on unfamiliar roads. Landslides and road repairs can close or slow roads at short notice, particularly after rain; check local conditions before setting out.
